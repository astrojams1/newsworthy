# Calibration

What the author says a reading should score, against what the rater gave it.
Each entry is a real stored reading. New versions are checked against this
list; an entry that a new version still gets wrong is the reason for the next
one.

## 2026-08-28 — every story type scored 5

The rater gave a median of 5 to every category measured over 72 hours:
humanitarian disaster, standing war, corporate settlement, trade policy,
military escalation. It was not discriminating between them at all.

| Reading | Rated | Author | Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Himalayan floods, 270 dead, 1,500 missing, India issues alerts | 5 | 3 | Distant catastrophe that changes nothing you do today is not a high score |
| Iran war day 181, ceasefire holds 28 nights, strait still crippled | 5 | 2-3 | A situation continuing without change is not a development |
| Meta settles child-safety suits for $17bn, must overhaul teen products | 5 | 3 | A large corporate settlement is business news, not news to act on |
| Canada imposes 15-50% retaliatory tariffs on $20bn of US goods | 5 | 3-4 | Each round of an ongoing dispute is incremental |

Two principles, both new in v8:

- **Novelty.** Severity that has not changed since yesterday is not news. This
  is judged intrinsically — "day 181", "ceasefire holds" — because readings are
  isolated and a caller cannot know what was rated before.
- **Reach.** A development with no bearing on the reader sits at 3 however
  grave it is elsewhere. The middle of the scale is reserved for what changes
  a decision.

Together they move a normal day from 5 to 3 and reclaim the top of the scale,
which 83% of readings had never reached.
