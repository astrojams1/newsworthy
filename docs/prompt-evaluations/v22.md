# v22: the sentence says who acted

On 2026-10-05 the owner asked why some sentences used the passive voice where
the active would read better, and to fix it. Shipped text: version 22, hash
`8916247a26173fdf`, 2,295 characters.

## The pattern in production

Passives are rare overall: about 5% of sentences from 2026-09-05 to 2026-10-05,
most of them harmless ("is expected", "were postponed"). The ones that hurt
cluster in one story. Under v20, over Oct 3 and 4, the Hormuz tanker attacks
came out with no attacker six times:

- 948: "An oil tanker was struck in the Strait of Hormuz on Oct 2, the sixth this week, …"
- 955, 961, 966, 978: "… oil tankers were reportedly hit near (the Strait of) Hormuz …"
- 986: "… as more tankers were struck near Hormuz, …"

The attacker was on record. Reading 989 from the same caller says "Iran … hit
another tanker Sunday", and a v21 test run on Oct 5 found that the
Revolutionary Guards had claimed the attacks.

**Why.** Under v19, "Claims: said or reportedly" replaced naming who reported
something. With an unconfirmed attack, "reportedly" plus a passive is the
shortest way to hedge, and nothing in Output asked for an actor beyond "who did
what". The forecast tail that v21 removed also used about 40 of the 135
characters.

## What changed from v21

Output only:

- "one development, who did what." became "one development, who did what in
  active voice, or who claims it."
- " on new developments" was cut from the label sentence to stay under rule 7.
  The rater submits no label either way.

Everything else is v21's verbatim. Rule 10 in `PROMPT-RULES.md` now requires the
active-voice clause in every later version. `scripts/passive-voice.mjs` flags a
passive with no actor in caller reviews.

## How it was tested

Claude Code subagents, the model the hourly caller runs (Opus 5.5), each fresh.
Nothing was submitted.

- P1–P3: tanker attacks, one claimed, one unclaimed, one disputed.
- P4: talks postponed by a named mediator.

version's Output.

v22 turned F2 into "A blast damaged an oil tanker" (both runs), and F4 into
"Oman, the mediator, postponed peace talks" (run B). Every claimed act names
its claimant under both versions. The notes state the claimant plainly, so the
fixtures cannot reproduce the production failure, where the caller found the
claim itself while researching. That is what the live runs below test.

The review flag also marks passives inside a clause whose actor the sentence
names ("the UK confirmed both were damaged"). Those are flags for a reader, not
findings.

## Limits

- The fixtures name every claimant. Whether the hourly caller names claimants
  it has to find itself is established only by naturally arriving v22
  readings. The caller review flags any agentless passive.
- Two live runs on one morning. Scores are not evidence about calibration, and
  the scale is unchanged.
