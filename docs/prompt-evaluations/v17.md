# v17: house style, so two readings are written the same way

The user asked for conventions that stop two readings of the same kind of news
being written differently. Their example was dates: always "Jan", never
"January" or "01". The reading live on 2026-09-24 did not follow it: "The
United States and China extended their tariff truce to January 10, so prices on
Chinese goods hold steady."

V17 makes two additions to v16 and changes nothing else.

- The one-number rule becomes "At most one figure, in plain terms; dates, years
  and ordinals don't count." A draft under the flat rule cut "first increase
  since 2023" to "first time in years".
- A closing style line: "Style: Jan 10, Oct (year only if not this one, no
  weekdays). US (not American), UK, EU, UN, NATO, the Fed. Digits: 3, 5%, $2
  billion, a quarter point, other currencies in dollars. Claims: said. US
  spelling, straight apostrophes."

The rest of Output is v16's verbatim, including its 135-character body and the
5 characters reserved for "New: ". Everything before Output is byte-identical,
so the scale, examples and calibration are unchanged. The prompt is 2,253
characters, under the 2,300 limit the owner approved for an earlier draft.

Three earlier drafts were dropped:

- One compressed v15's wording to fit under 2,000.
- One moved the conventions into a reference file served beside the prompt.
  The owner rejected it because the file would grow without a limit to check
  it.
- One wrote the style line at more length (2,292 characters). It had no rule
  for bare months or other currencies, and a test of it wrote "800 million
  euros". The owner asked for the conventions to be written as efficiently as
  possible. The terse line covers both gaps in 39 fewer characters.

## Where the catalog comes from

Stored production readings sit behind the admin token, which this session did
not have. The token is in the owner's 1Password vault (see `AGENTS.md`). The
catalog draws on the sentences the repository keeps:

- the live reading above;
- every generated output in the v12–v16 evaluation records, more than 200 outputs
  from GPT-6 Astra and Claude;
- the readings quoted in `AGENTS.md`.

Repeating the tallies against `/api/admin/history` would show how often each
inconsistency occurs in production.

## Evidence

[Exact instructions, inputs and every output](v17.json) compare v16 and v17 on
12 source cards. Eight are reused from v15.json. The other four are new and
built to test conventions: `truce`, reconstructed from the live reading, plus
`eu-fine`, `shutdown` and `boe`. The notes in the new cards deliberately use
long forms, full dates, weekdays, "percent" and "euros".

Each arm ran twice. Each run was a fresh Claude Opus subagent with no web
search, standing in for the production rater and its research workflow.
Scoring was not requested, so these results say nothing about calibration.

| Arm | Sentences | Characters | Mean | Within 135 | Sentences breaking a convention |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| v16 | 24 | 110–130 | 123 | 24/24 | 5 |
| v17 | 24 | 108–135 | 126 | 24/24 | 0 |

The v16 breaches were "January 2027", "Sept 30", "the Federal Reserve" and
"October" in two outputs. Both v17 runs wrote:

- "Jan 10, 2027", with the year because it is not the current year;
- "Sep 30", "Oct 1" and "the Fed's Oct meeting";
- "the Fed", "$930 million" for the euro fine, and "a quarter point";
- "since 2023" and "third cut" beside a figure.

v17 sentences run 3 characters longer on average, and two reach exactly 135,
so none exceed the body budget. The v16 runs are reused from the first v17
comparison, since v16 and the cards are unchanged.

v15 broke conventions in 14 sentences in the first comparison, against v16's
5 here, so some of the drift varies from run to run. The size of the gain is
uncertain, but its direction is consistent.

The review against the cards found one cost. "Meta said it will appeal"
appears in both arms and mixes tenses.

Neither arm invented a fact. No outputs were repaired or truncated.

Two runs per arm cannot give a compliance rate, and the new cards were written
to exercise the rules they test. Whether live sentences follow the style depends
on callers fetching v17 and returning a verified digest. That adoption has not
yet been established.
