# v18: the score is market risk

On 2026-09-26 the owner defined the score: the current risk of a sharp market
move, up or down, with the reading naming the development most likely to cause
one. They also asked that the search not work from a fixed list of sites, so
that it can find breakout stories.

v9 to v17 measured something else. Their Summary was "Rate how much the current
news warrants attention right now", and their rungs were steps of personal
impact, from "Touches you indirectly" to "Act today". v18 is a new instrument,
and readings under v17 and v18 are not comparable (the same was true of v5).

Shipped text: version 18, hash `e134aa373968ebed`, 2,259 characters.

## What changed from v17

- **Summary** defines the score as market risk, up or down, and asks the rater
  to name the development most likely to cause a move.
- **Sources** ask for:
  - what broke in the last few hours, searched by time and topic rather than a
    fixed list of sites;
  - the latest moves in futures, bond yields, oil, the dollar, volatility and
    bitcoin, where a sharp move with no reported cause counts as a lead;
  - prices read from a dated quote page, not a search snippet;
  - prediction markets for one-day moves only, at most two searches.

  Hacker News is dropped. v6's lag guard stays: markets are shut two thirds of
  the time, and their silence is not evidence against an event.
- **Scale:** ten market-risk rungs. The owner approved the wording.
- **Examples:** five examples, scored and approved by the owner. Each is a
  real stored reading shortened to a headline. The flood example carries over
  from v9.
- **Output:** v17's, with two changes. The sentence now describes "what it
  could mean for prices or savings" rather than "what it means for daily life".
  The clause about revising after the history match is gone, because the
  sentence is now final before the record is fetched.

## How it was tested

The tests used Claude Code subagents, the same kind of model the hourly caller
runs (Opus 5.5). Each subagent was fresh and got only the prompt, WebSearch,
and `curl` to read pages. They never saw the other runs or our history. These
are not production runs: nothing was submitted, and the caller's judging and
record steps were not part of them.

There were two kinds of test:

- **Live runs:** the agent researched the news at the time it ran.
- **Fixture scoring:** the agent scored real stored readings as if each one
  were the top development of its moment. It had no web access. This tests how
  the scale spreads its scores; it does not test search.

### Round 1: first draft, live, Sat 2026-09-26 23:27 UTC (markets shut)

Three runs on the draft and one control on v17, all started at the same moment.

| Run | Score | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| v18 A | 5 | Trump said he rejected Iran's plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which could push oil and gas prices back up when markets reopen. |
| v18 B | 5 | Trump said he rejected Iran's plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a key oil route, which could lift fuel prices when trading resumes. |
| v18 C | 5 | Trump rejected Iran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a key oil shipping route, so oil and gas prices could rise again next week. |
| v17 control | 4 | Trump rejected Iran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within a week, leaving a key oil route mostly shut and fuel costs uncertain. |

All three v18 runs chose the story for a market reason. Friday's roughly 2% oil
drop had been priced on hopes of the deal, and the rejection came after the
close. The v17 control weighed a regional nor'easter as a serious rival, which
makes sense for reader attention and has no bearing on markets.

Problems found:

- Every price came from a search snippet. None of the runs checked the dollar
  or volatility. One snippet quoted oil prices from an earlier episode; run A
  caught the mismatch.
- The Output section still carried the stale history-match clause.

Fixes: prices must come from a dated quote page, and the stale clause was
removed.

### Round 2: fixture scoring, first scale

Two agents scored 12 stored readings. Their scores appear as X and Y in the
table under round 4. They agreed exactly on 10 of 12.

Problem found: the Fed's first hike in three years scored 7 (X) and 6 (Y), even
though another item in the same set said markets had priced it at 92%. The
scale had no way to tell a surprise from something already priced in.

Fix: rung 2 became "Known or priced-in risks; nothing new." (it had been "Known
risks only; nothing changed today."). Rung 7 became "Unexpected shock now;
sharp moves likely." (it had been "Shock breaking now; sharp moves likely.").

### Round 3: revised scale

Fixture scoring by two agents (P and Q). Two items were added to the set:

- F13, a surprise emergency hike;
- F14, a large bank failure.

F7 was restated to say the hike had been priced at 92%.

The priced hike (F7) dropped to 2 from both agents. The surprise hike scored 7
and 8, and the bank failure 8. The agents agreed exactly on 12 of 14.

Two live runs followed, at 23:31 UTC with markets shut:

| Run | Score | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| D | 4 | Trump said he rejected Iran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz in 7 days, which could push oil prices back up when trading resumes. |
| E | 4 | Trump said he rejected Iran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which could push oil and gas prices back up when trading resumes. |

Both runs read prices from dated pages. D pulled a dated quote for every named
instrument from Yahoo Finance's chart data: Brent, WTI, S&P futures, the
10-year yield, VIX, the dollar index and bitcoin. E used an AP close story and
a quote strip on a page. E also threw out two May articles that search had
presented as current.

Both scored 4, not round 1's 5, because they weighed a calm VIX of 14.9 and a
war markets have long priced. This is the effect the "priced-in" wording was
meant to have, and the one-point difference is within the rater's normal
disagreement.

discarded. The quote-page instruction was followed where a page existed, but
not everywhere.

## Limits

- **Live runs happened only on Saturday night, while markets were shut.** How
  well the prompt catches a breakout on a trading day is untested. The first
  weekday runs after release are the real test.
- **Nothing in our history scored above 6.** The top of the scale is supported
  only by the rung wording and the two hypothetical fixtures.
- **The runs above are tests, not production readings.** Adoption will be
  established by a naturally arriving reading stamped v18 with
  `prompt_verified: true`.
- **The public tagline, "World news, rated by significance", predates v18.**
  So does the App Store copy, "Higher scores mean more consequential
  developments". Whether to change them is a separate decision for the owner.
