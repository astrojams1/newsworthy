# v25: room for an "Update: " label

The app will label a sentence either **New:**, for a development in a new
story, or **Update:**, for a newer development in a story already on record
(owner's decision, 2026-11-14). v16 to v24 reserved 5 of the 140 display
characters for "New: " and allowed a 135-character sentence. "Update: " takes
8, so a v24 sentence of 133 to 135 characters overflows the display under the
new label. Shipped text: version 25, hash `8ad7f86da493eaea`, 2,288
characters.

## What changed from v24

Output only, two edits:

- `"<one sentence, at most 135 characters>"` became
  `"<one sentence, at most 132 characters>"`.
- `5 for the app's "New: " label.` became `8 for the app's label.` The rater
  cannot know which label the app will show, so the label is no longer named
  and the reserve covers the longer one.

Everything before Output is byte-identical to v24 (checked by comparing the
rendered texts up to the Output heading). Scale and Examples are untouched.
This is a writing-only change; nothing here bears on calibration.

## How it was tested

A substitute for the production rater, not the rater itself. Production runs
`claude-opus-5` (`DEFAULT_MODEL` in `src/rate.js`) with its own research. Here
each generation was a fresh Claude Code subagent (Agent tool,
`general-purpose` type, `opus` model alias; the record does not pin the model
version). Generation settings were the harness defaults: the Agent tool
exposes no temperature, sampling or token settings. Agents had no research
step: each read one file holding the full `renderPrompt(24)` or
`renderPrompt(25)` text and one fixture note, with an instruction to use no
tool beyond reading that file and to reply with only the JSON. Nothing was
submitted to the API.

Eleven fixtures, three independent generations each per version, 66 in all;
each generation ran in its own subagent and saw no other output.

- D1 to D8: [v24-fixtures.md](v24-fixtures.md), unchanged.
- N1 to N3: added for v25 to tempt a long sentence. They are reconstructed
  notes built from Nov 13 to 14 developments as given in the task (the FAA
  halt of all US departures and its lifting at 8:15 p.m. Eastern on Nov 13;
  the US military's account of shooting down 2 Iranian drones headed for
  Bahrain and denying Iran's radar claim; bitcoin falling below $67,000), not
  independently verified reporting.

[v25-runs.md](v25-runs.md) holds the exact preamble, the fixtures, every
output verbatim with its agent id, and the raw JSON. No output was edited,
repaired or truncated.

Measured with a Node script: characters as Unicode code points
(`[...s].length`), each version's own limit, and the label plus sentence
against 140 with "Update: " (n + 8) and "New: " (n + 5). Em dashes,
semicolons, curly quotes, outlet names and relative day words were checked by
pattern, then every sentence was read against its note.

## Findings

**The change does what it is for.** v24, unchanged, would overflow the
display under "Update: " in about a quarter of these sentences (8 of 33),
because it writes up to its 135 limit. v25 stayed within 132 every time and
fit both labels every time. It used the new room the way v24 used the old:
four v25 sentences ran 130 to 131.

**Cost: a few characters of detail.** Without the one-line bitcoin fixture,
v25 sentences averaged about 5 characters shorter. What fell out was
secondary: the Fed's Oct 28 meeting in D1 (v24 2 of 3, v25 1 of 3), "this
year" in D7 (v24 3 of 3, v25 1 of 3), Dhahran in D2 (v24 2 of 3, v25 1 of 3),
and "in 2 days" once in D8. The core development and its attribution
survived in every v25 sentence. In N1, v25 led with the halt being lifted and
gave the stated cause in all three runs (v24 gave the cause in 1 of 3, and
called the event a "network failure" in 2).

**Fidelity, attribution and grammar.** No sentence contradicts its note.
Points to note, none attributable to the change at this sample size:

- N2: two v24 runs wrote "denied Iran's claim that it destroyed a US radar",
  where "it" can read as the US military. All three v25 runs were
  unambiguous ("claim to have destroyed", "that Iran destroyed").
- N1: "FAA" appears unexplained in 1 v24 and 2 v25 sentences; the others say
  "US aviation regulators". v25 run 3, "halted and later lifted a stop on all
  US departures", is awkward.
- D1 v25 run 3 says the figures will be "released Oct 14", which reads as
  past tense for a future release.
- D8 v24 run 1 says "near Hormuz" where the note says "in the Strait of
  Hormuz"; D3 v24 run 3 adds "US" to the Gulf output, an inference.
- N3 v24 run 1 is redundant ("fell below $67,000 on Nov 14, a drop in the
  largest digital currency").
- N3 dates: given "now Nov 14, 04:00 UTC" and "in the last few hours", one
  v24 and two v25 runs dated the fall Nov 14, which is right in UTC but
  evening Nov 13 in the US. This is the UTC dating question v24 recorded, in
  both versions.

**Relative time.** v25 N3 run 3 kept the note's "in the last few hours". It is
not a relative day word, so it breaks no rule as written, but it ages the same
way "today" did in v24's incident. One occurrence, copied from a note that
offered no time, in a version whose wording on dates is v24's.

**Market predictions.** None. D6 sentences reported the futures pricing in
the note as odds or traders' expectations of a Fed rate rise (v24 3 of 3, v25
2 of 3), the pattern v24's record accepted as reported pricing rather than a
forecast of a market move. v25 run 3's "so traders expect" states a causal
link the note implies but does not say.

**Scores** varied a little from the fixture's chosen score in both versions
(D4 and D8 in each). The scale is unchanged, and fixtures with a chosen score
are not evidence about calibration.

## Limits

- A substitute model with no research step. Whether the hourly caller stays
  within 132 is established only by naturally arriving v25 readings with
  `prompt_verified: true`.
- Three generations per fixture per version is too few for a compliance
  rate; the length result is clear only because v24 overflowed 8 times and
  v25 never did.
- N1 to N3 are reconstructed from a task brief, not from source reporting.
- Fixtures state the current time, so they cannot test how the caller dates
  its own research (the v24 lesson).
- The test measures the 140-character budget as the prompt states it. It does
  not check how the app renders either label, or whether "Update:" is shown
  for the right readings.
