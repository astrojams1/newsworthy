# v21: the sentence never predicts a market move

On 2026-10-05 the owner asked for the end-of-sentence market commentary to stop
("…, which could lift oil and fuel prices"). Shipped text: version 21, hash
`3f042c2d18d27cb3`, 2,281 characters.

## The pattern in production

All stored readings, 2026-09-05 to 2026-10-05, read through the admin history.
"Forecast tail" means the last clause hedges (could, may, might, likely, would)
about prices, markets or borrowing costs.

Under v20, 85 of 126 readings contain "which/that/so … could lift/push …", and

**Cause.** v18 changed Output's "who did what and what it means for daily life"
to "who did what and what it could mean for prices or savings". The rater did
as asked. v19 and v20 kept the phrase.

## What changed from v20

Output only. "one development, who did what and what it could mean for prices
or savings." became "one development, who did what. Never predict market
moves." Everything else is v20's verbatim, so v20 and v21 readings are the same
instrument. A move that happened may still be reported.

A first draft ("Name a market move only if it happened; never predict one.")
was 2,315 characters, over rule 7; a second ("Report market moves that
happened; predict none.") was 2,302. Neither was tested.

## How it was tested

Nothing was submitted. The judging and record steps were not part of the tests.

## Limits

- Fixture notes are reconstructed from stored readings, not independently
  checked reporting.
- Four live runs on one Monday morning. Whether the hourly caller stops adding
  forecasts is established only by naturally arriving v21 readings with
  `prompt_verified: true`; the caller review now flags any forecast
  (`scripts/market-forecast.mjs`).
- The production table counts only forecasts in the last clause. A sentence
  that opens with one ("US jobs data due today could jolt stocks…") is not
  counted there, so those rates are a floor. The review flag checks every
  clause, but it is a heuristic: a hedge and a market word in one clause.
