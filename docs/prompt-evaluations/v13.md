# v13 widget sentence budget

The user reported a clipped medium-widget sentence on 20 September 2026.
The public `/api/current` response matched the screenshot and contained 148
characters including spaces and punctuation:

> Saudi Arabia said its air defenses shot down a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh, the first attack on the capital since the Iran war widened.

The 150-character budget permits this length. v13 lowers it to 100 characters,
cutting the permitted length by a third. Everything else in the prompt is byte-identical to v12. It still asks for complete sentences,
plain language, attribution and useful specifics. Caller and OpenAPI descriptions
use the same budget. API validation and historical readings are unchanged.

## Evaluation

[Exact inputs, instructions, raw responses and measured outputs](v13.json) retain
a comparison of v12 and v13 on the same seven source cards: the six reconstructed
v12 cases plus the reported Riyadh sentence. Three fresh CLI invocations per arm
produce 21 sentences per arm. An additional v13 invocation was started while
certificate failures appeared to block the first batch; its seven outputs are
retained separately, not substituted for any failed or unfavorable result.
The configured model was GPT-6 Astra at high effort, not the production caller.

The wrapper requested source-notes-only writing without tools. Some invocations
nevertheless searched and added source links. The raw responses retain these
additions. This deviates from the intended controlled offline evaluation;
results demonstrate these actual generations, not a clean isolated comparison
or verified reporting. Source cards are reconstructed writing fixtures, never
rating-calibration examples. Outputs were not repaired or truncated.

All seven additional v13 outputs were also within 100 characters (73–96).
Manual review found no clear factual or grammatical failure against the supplied
notes. Saudi and Polish attribution survived, tariffs remained authorized rather
than imposed, and pipeline restoration remained a future plan. The shorter
budget removed China and India from the sanctions sentence in all three v13
runs, and dropped secondary rates details. This is the intended space tradeoff,
not evidence that every shorter sentence carries the same amount of information.

The Riyadh outputs were 88, 88 and 74 characters. Both prompts sometimes produced
the same short sentence, so this does not show that v12 always writes too much;
v13 narrows the allowed maximum. No universal compliance or device-fit guarantee
follows from this small sample.

## Verification boundary

The user's screenshot establishes clipping of the old sentence. This change
reduces the permitted length; character count alone cannot guarantee layout fit
across glyph widths, score 10, widget sizes or accessibility text settings.
No new WidgetKit, Android launcher or physical-device verification was performed.
The native layout and font sizes are unchanged. Full native fit remains unverified.

The public current endpoint does not expose prompt verification. Authorized
history and live prompt credentials were unavailable in this shell, so the
reported reading cannot be attributed to v12 by digest here. It is evidence that
a sentence within v12's allowed budget clips, not proof of which prompt generated
it. Deployment, any production `NEWSWORTHY_PROMPT_VERSION` override, and adoption
by a naturally arriving verified v13 reading remain release checks. Existing
saved readings will not become shorter merely because the prompt changes.
