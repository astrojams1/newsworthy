# v15: calm, plain sentence for a non-expert reader

The user found live readings harsh, dense and written for experts. The reading
live on 2026-09-24 illustrates it: "The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.12%, a
19-year high, raising loan costs as October Fed hike odds rose to 71%." It stacks
three figures and two terms of art in one sentence.

V15 changes only the writing guidance in Output. Everything before Output is
byte-identical to v14, so the scale, examples and calibration are unchanged. The
120-character body and 140-character display budget are unchanged. V14's "Keep
useful specifics" is replaced by: write calmly for a reader with no background,
say what the development means for daily life, use at most one number in plain
terms, explain or drop jargon, market figures and uncommon acronyms, and avoid
dramatic verbs. The full prompt is 1,956 characters.

Review against the cards: v15 replaced "10-year Treasury yield", "Brent",
"quarter point to 3.75%-4%" and "deniable" with plain equivalents, and added
context a non-expert lacks ("a key oil shipping route"). It also kept caveats
v14 had dropped: tariffs not yet imposed, Polish-alleged strikes not yet
happened, Greenland scale unspecified. Costs: exact figures such as 5.12% and
$102 are gone, sentences run about 13 characters longer and one reached exactly
120, and one output rendered a 71% market probability as "markets bet on a Fed
rate rise", which sounds more certain than the notes. No outputs were repaired or
truncated.

Two runs per arm is too small for a compliance rate. Whether the live rater's
sentences read as calmer depends on callers fetching v15 and returning a
verified digest; that adoption is not yet established.
